Finish Common, Accounting, and Orders — then the money path to Sales.

The engines are further along than the screens. Orders can book, price, tax, capture, refund, renew, and reverse in a live database. Accounting can stage a balanced subledger and pretend to post it. Common has a real Person and Organization. What we do not have is a family that feels finished: People do not grow extra life when Orders is installed, Accounting still looks like a journal-entry app, most Orders mockups already have a rail page (thin, not missing), Sales and Contracts are specified (and partly built) on other branches, and the integration suite does not yet walk the same GraphQL path the Angular forms use.

Thesis

Three jobs, not thirty screens

  • Common is the shared identity graph. Its generated (and custom) forms must be extensible by other apps so a Person grows orders, tickets, deals, and contracts when those apps are installed — without Common importing any of them.
  • Accounting is a subledger + dispatch bridge to NetSuite / Business Central / QBO. The JE workspace is an exception door, not the product.
  • Orders is the transaction engine and the first finished Explorer surface. Close the remaining order-taker path, then deepen the operator rails that already exist (list, dashboard, fulfillment, payments, returns). Finish the few screens still mock-only (statements, Confirm pre-flight, document-as-nav). Do not invent a second entry lane.

Sales and Contracts sit on top of this. They cannot close a real deal into a real bill until Orders grows two small seams and Common grows an Activity spine. That is why finishing these three is how the other two become shippable.

Auto-extend People / Orgs

People and Organizations are generic on purpose. When Orders is installed, opening Jane Doe should show her tickets, orders, subscriptions, and bill-to history. When Sales is installed, her deals. Common must not import either app.

The trap we already fell in

Common’s custom Person / Org forms replace the generated ones. Generated forms emit <mj-form-panel-slot> hosts. The custom templates do not. So the one plugin mechanism MJ already has is dead on the two entities that matter most. Generated related-entity grids (Orders bill-to, Tasks, etc.) are also gone — they were baked into Common’s HTML at Common’s CodeGen time, and OpenApp install never rewrites that HTML.

Proposal — do this, in this order

B · this week · no MJ change

1. Put the slots back. 2. Orders ships panels.

Restore before-fields / after-fields / after-related on Common’s custom Person and Org templates (and keep after-everything from the container). Then Orders registers three BaseFormPanels against MJ_BizApps_Common: People and …Organizations:

  • Orders as bill-to / ship-to (grid, newest first)
  • Event tickets (Person only)
  • Subscriptions

Client bootstrap already loads orders-ng when the app is installed. Common stays ignorant. Sales later does the same for deals.

A · next MJ PR · platform

Runtime related-entity host

A container that, at runtime, renders every RelatedEntities row with DisplayInForm using the existing grid + BuildRelationshipViewParams. Dedup against already-baked panels.

Then a seeded EntityRelationship from Orders is enough for a default grid. Custom cards (tickets) still use panels. This is the thing people already believe DisplayInForm does.

Do not start a new MJ: Form Extensions catalog until A exists and we still need toolbar verbs / hero replace. Those are real, and they are a later conversation (proposal E). Do not let Orders replace Common’s Person form class.

Broader concept: an installed OpenApp contributes capabilities onto entities it does not own. Three layers, same idea as permissions:

  • Metadata — relationships, DisplayInForm, maybe later a form-extension row. Default grid for free.
  • Panels — Angular in the contributing app, ClassFactory onto a slot. Custom UX.
  • Actions / toolbar — not discovered today. Only add when “Create order from this Person” is a real verb we will not put inside a panel.

Accounting as a bridge

Accounting is not a place to type journal entries. It is the AR subledger of record that stages, nets, locks, and posts what Orders (and later Payments, Payroll) already decided, into the customer’s GL. The Zuora→NetSuite / Stripe→QBO analogy is the product.

1. Orders booksOne JE per line on Confirm. Role-mapped accounts. Provenance on the line.
2. Subledger holdsPending, balanced, immutable once batched. Detail stays here forever.
3. Batch + approveNet by company × account × dim. Tasks CFO gate. Real today.
4. DispatchOne summarized JE per company to BC / NS / QBO. Mock poster only.
5. ERP is the GLPeriods, statements, close. We store ExternalAccountID + the batch ref.

Integration suite — is it real?

Short answer: Orders’ engine suite is real and has already paid for itself (eight product defects before UI existed). It is not yet the Angular path. Accounting is closer to the wire on the batch side and has no unified mj test catalog.

Orders today

  • Harness: @mj-biz-apps/orders-integration-tests registered on IntegrationCheckRegistry. Run via mj test suite "BizApps Orders Integration" or node test-harnesses/integration.mjs.
  • 000 / world: catalog-world (CW1) commits people, orgs, catalog, GL from CSV. That is the setup. Then ~32 bundles: booking, rev-rec, subs, cancel, renew, payments, IC, events, pricing, promotions, charges, tax, composition, returns, gift cards, bundles, fulfillment, capture, invoicing, ACH, entitlements, concurrency, volume…
  • How it writes: entity API (order-builder.ts) so OrderEntityServer.Save / Confirm() fire. Assertions via provider.ExecuteSQL against Orders and Accounting tables. Each check is RequiresMutation and rolls back.
  • Transport: server. The fixture says so on purpose — a client transport has no transaction to roll back. Comments in volume / concurrency refuse client runs.

The gap you named

Explorer talks GraphQL. Companions (InitialPaymentReference), related-record collections, Confirm() over the wire, ClassFactory registration on the client, and “save header + lines + extension in one trip” are exactly the seams a server-side builder never touches. A green server suite can hide a form that cannot book.

Keep

Server engine suite

Fast, transactional, found real bugs, asserts ledger state. Do not rewrite it as GraphQL. Do not drop RUN_MUTATION_TESTS=1 (without it the driver skips everything and reports success). Phase 5 CI (rebuild-db → mj test) is still open and still worth it.

Add

A GraphQL UX twin

Same world, same assertions, different door. Talk to a running MJAPI as the Angular form does: GraphQLDataProvider or ExecuteRemoteOperation. Prefix-and-sweep teardown (MJ’s client pattern) because there is no outer transaction. This is the suite that would have caught a companion that never crossed the wire.

Accounting today

A 4-tier local pyramid, not mj test:

  • T2 server — live DB, trigger proofs, engine-runtime re-reads SQL after CreateJournalEntry.
  • T3 API — GraphQL ExecuteRemoteOperation with an API key. full-flow-api.ts is company → COA → 3 JEs → preview/build/approve/dispatch → assert GLPosted. This is the shape we want.
  • T5 Playwright — shell flows. Useful, not a substitute for headless GraphQL.

Doc drift is bad (READMEs name deleted harnesses). There is no 000. There is no Orders→Accounting family bundle on the GraphQL path. Fold the surviving T3 scripts into mj test check modules and add one cross-app bundle. Keep Playwright for chrome, not for ledger truth.

Vacuous-green rules (both repos)

  • A refusal check must assert the message, not just Save() === false (Orders already learned this on OB7–OB9).
  • assert-check-count must fail if fewer checks ran than the registry declares.
  • GraphQL suite must fail if MJAPI is down — never skip-as-pass.
  • Posted to an ERP is not proven until the poster is not mockErpPoster.

What not to do

  • Do not rebuild Fast Entry + a full editor. One form. The mock index still points at Fast Entry — update the index when we delete it.
  • Do not put booked order JEs in the Accounting workspace. Do not grow JE compose.
  • Do not let Orders @RegisterClass a Person form. Panels only.
  • Do not treat the server integration suite as proof the form works. Add the GraphQL twin; do not replace the engine suite.
  • Do not start NetSuite and QBO posters in parallel with BC.
  • Do not implement event capacity, subscription seat quantity, or IC settlement without a ruling — they are real and they are large.
  • Do not flip Entity Action bindings Pending → Active until MJ’s transition filter exists.
  • Do not publish Accounting 0.1.0 from main until there is a real release. Linking is the product until then.

Definition of “these three apps are finished enough”

  • A Person with Orders installed shows tickets / orders / subscriptions without Common knowing why.
  • An order taker can pick an org, add people as lines, take a check number, and Confirm — and a GraphQL test books the same shape into the ledger.
  • Those operator screens already exist — they look like the job (seed + pre-flight + statements), not a thin count tile.
  • A CFO can map Harbor’s DefRev to a BC account and dispatch a batch that is not MOCK-.
  • Contracts can mark a subscription External and have SpawnRenewals skip it. Sales can close onto that without stubbing.
