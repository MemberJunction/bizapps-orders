A Subscriptions tab with a Renewals page. Every subscription whose coverage ends inside a chosen window (30 days to 12 months ahead, plus terms that ended in the last 30 days without renewing), soonest first, with the customer, account, company, product, start and end dates, status, the auto-renew switch, the order that bought the current term and its price, and the renewal: the renewal order where one exists (number, date, status, price, booked or still a draft), otherwise the price a renewal would be placed at and the day the nightly pass will place it. Tick rows and create their renewal orders: the page previews through the renewal pass, shows what will be placed and skipped, and asks whether to leave the orders as drafts (the default) or confirmed and invoiced at once. It warns when a sibling from the same original order was left out.

"Change product" corrects the product on the ticked subscriptions through the new `Orders.ChangeSubscriptionProduct` (the subscription is what the customer has now; booked orders and deals keep what was sold; a different subscription type is refused unless the dialog's "also move" is ticked). The next renewal carries the corrected product at the current price plus increase.

`Orders.SpawnRenewals` now places ONE renewal order per customer per renewal date: subscriptions with the same selling company, holder and beneficiary, expiring term end, placement and renewal card go on one order with a line each (`RenewalGroupKey`), inside one transaction. A product added mid-term joins only when co-terminated; a line carrying promotion codes renews alone. `MaxCount` and `Placed` count orders; each candidate reports its `OrderGroup`. The scheduled pass produces fewer, combined orders for customers who bought several products together. Company and Product dropdowns (`mjo-multi-select`, new) narrow the list to what is in the window, with counts; the increase sits under every renewal price (projected, or actual against the current price once placed); and Export downloads the rows shown as Excel or CSV in one click through MJ's export service. Every row can be ticked: "Create renewal orders" acts on the ticked rows that can renew, "Auto-renew" turns the switch on or off for all of them, and "Update increase %" sets `RenewalIncreasePercent`, both through the new `Orders.SetRenewalSettings`, which writes subscriptions only and never an order. The side panel shows each subscription's own rate or what it inherits, with the same dialog for one. Dates on the page are MM/DD/YYYY (`FormatDate` gains a `Numeric` option).

A new subscription with no rate of its own takes the negotiated annual increase from the contract behind its birth order (`SubscriptionEntityServer` on create, through order line → order → deal → contract; `ContractIncrease.ts`). The lookup is optional: nothing happens on a host without the Sales and Contracts apps, and a failed lookup is logged and never blocks a confirm.

New read-only operation `Orders.GetRenewalWorklist`. `Orders.SpawnRenewals` takes `SubscriptionIDs` (place renewals for the subscriptions a person chose, whatever the lead window or the switch say, with every ineligible choice reported) and `PlaceAs` (`Confirmed` or `Draft`); its pricing moved to a shared `RenewalPricer` so the page's projection and the pass's order are one figure. The scheduled pass itself is unchanged. The subscription register moved from the Receivables rail to the new tab, and `mjo-worklist-table` gained row ticking (`Selectable`, `SelectedKeys`, `SelectionChanged`).

The Orders application's `DefaultNavItems` gain the Subscriptions tab (`metadata/applications/.orders-application.json`); like every metadata change this reaches a host through the release's metadata sync migration, not through this package alone.
